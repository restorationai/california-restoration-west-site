## Why El Rio Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of El Rio's housing stock sits on or near agricultural land with a historically high water table, and the flat grading throughout the area means storm water and irrigation runoff have fewer places to go. Homes and outbuildings on or near former farmland often have older plumbing tied into slab foundations, and a slow leak under a slab can travel for weeks before it surfaces as a soft spot in the flooring or a musty smell in a closet. Properties closer to the river corridor also face a higher risk of saturated soil pushing moisture up through foundations during the wet season, which is a different mechanism than a straightforward roof or appliance leak and needs to be diagnosed, not just mopped up.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in El Rio

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted equipment before it has a chance to wick further into drywall, baseboards, and subflooring. From there we map moisture with meters, not guesswork, checking slab, framing, and any crawl space access to find out how far the water actually traveled versus how far it looks like it traveled. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed to pull moisture out of materials methodically rather than just blowing air around a room. In homes with slab foundations common throughout El Rio, we pay close attention to drying timelines because concrete releases moisture slowly, and closing up a room too early is how hidden mold problems start six weeks later.
