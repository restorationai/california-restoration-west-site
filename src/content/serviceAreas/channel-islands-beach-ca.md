## Restoration emergencies common in Channel Islands Beach

Channel Islands Beach sits right where the Santa Clara River floodplain meets the Pacific, and the harbor canals that define the community also define its risk profile. Homes close to the water deal with higher ambient humidity year-round, which keeps drying times longer than a job of the same size would take a few miles inland. Winter storms moving through the Ventura County coastline bring wind-driven rain that finds its way through aging roof flashing and stucco cracks, and king tides combined with heavy surf can push water back through storm drains into low-lying yards and garages. Because so much of the building stock here sits at or near sea level, even a modest rain event can mean standing water against foundations longer than homeowners expect.

## Services we provide in Channel Islands Beach

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Channel Islands Beach

Most homes in Channel Islands Beach date to the 1960s and 1970s, built slab-on-grade with wood-frame walls and stucco or wood siding, a combination that handles coastal wind well but gives water very few places to go once it gets past the slab edge or through a roof penetration. Plumbing from that era is a mix of galvanized steel and early copper, both of which corrode faster in a salt-air environment than they would inland, and that corrosion is a common source of slow leaks that go unnoticed until flooring or drywall shows damage. Soil here is sandy and sits close to the water table given the proximity to the harbor channels, which means crawlspaces and sub-slab areas can stay damp longer after a storm than the surface conditions suggest. Structural repairs and rebuilds fall under Ventura County and City of Oxnard-area permitting depending on the parcel, and any scope touching older plumbing or roofing should plan for inspection before materials close back up.
