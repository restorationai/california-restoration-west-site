Ask for certification names, not just a logo on the website, and get the estimate in writing before any demolition begins.

## What certifications should a fire damage restoration company in Ventura have?

Look for a company that holds IICRC certification and can name its specific credentials on request. The [IICRC's S700 standard](https://iicrc.org/s700/) governs professional fire and smoke damage restoration, so it's worth asking which of a company's certifications apply to fire and smoke work versus water damage.

California Restoration West holds IICRC WRT (water restoration technician), AMRT (mold), and ASD (applied structural drying) certifications, and is recognized as an IICRC Certified Firm with OSHA-trained staff. When you call a company, ask directly: "What IICRC certifications does your crew hold, and can I see the firm's certification number?" A company that hesitates or changes the subject is telling you something.

Certification matters because fire restoration isn't just cleanup. Soot is acidic and keeps etching glass, metal, and finishes the longer it sits. A trained crew knows which cleaning agents neutralize it versus which ones just smear it around, and when a surface needs to be replaced instead of cleaned.

## Is the company insured, and do they have the right equipment for smoke and soot?

A legitimate fire restoration company carries general liability insurance and should be willing to provide proof of it before work begins. Ask to see a certificate of insurance, not just hear "yes, we're insured" over the phone.

Equipment is the other tell. Soot and smoke odor don't come out with a mop and an air freshener. You're looking for HEPA air scrubbers to pull particulates out of the air, thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators for odor that's worked into porous materials, and separate protocols for wet smoke versus dry smoke residue. If a company's answer to "how do you handle the smoke smell" is vague, that's a sign they're subcontracting the hard part or skipping it. California Restoration West's [smoke damage restoration](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) and [odor removal](/services/odor-removal/) work both follow this layered approach: contain the space, remove the particulate, then treat what's left in the materials.

## What should a written estimate actually include?

A real fire damage estimate is a line-item scope of work, not a single lump-sum number scribbled on a business card. It should list what's being cleaned versus replaced, by room, with enough detail that your insurance adjuster can review it directly against the claim.

Before any demolition starts, you want documentation: photos of the damage, moisture and soot readings where relevant, and a scope that separates mitigation (stopping further damage) from full restoration (rebuild and reconstruction). This paper trail is what protects you if the adjuster questions a line item later, and it's also what [Insurance Information Institute guidance](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance) points to as the standard for settling a disaster claim cleanly: documented, itemized, and tied to the actual loss.

If you want the general shape of how a fire loss moves from first call to final rebuild, the [fire damage restoration process](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-process/) breaks down each phase in order. It's worth reading before you sign a scope of work so you know what should already be on it.

## How do I check reviews and local reputation?

Check the company's Google Business Profile rating and read a handful of the actual reviews, not just the star average. California Restoration West currently holds a 5.0 rating from its Google reviews, which you can verify directly on the listing rather than taking anyone's word for it.

Look specifically for reviews that mention fire or smoke jobs, not just water damage or mold, since those are different skill sets. A company can be excellent at water extraction and still be new to soot cleaning, so match the reviews to the job you actually have.

## What happens on the first call with a fire restoration company?

The first call should cover the extent of the damage, whether the home is currently safe to enter, and what documentation you need before cleanup starts. A company worth hiring will walk you through containment and board-up needs for any exposed openings before they talk about full restoration.

From there, expect a scheduled walkthrough to assess soot penetration, water used by the fire department, and structural areas affected by heat. The [U.S. Fire Administration's guidance on recovering after a home fire](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/after-a-fire/) is a useful independent reference for what to expect in those first days, including what to do about utilities and salvageable belongings.
