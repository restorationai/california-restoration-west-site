**TL;DR:** A burst pipe in Ventura means two calls, not one: a plumber to stop the leak at its source, and a water damage restoration company to begin extraction and drying before mold sets in. Shut off your main water valve first, then make both calls within the first hour. Drying needs to start within hours, not days, to protect your home and your insurance claim.

If a pipe just split inside the wall of your Midtown Ventura bungalow, or water is spreading across the floor of a home near the Pierpont neighborhood, you don't have time for research. You have minutes before water reaches your baseboards, your subfloor, and anything stored at floor level. Stopping the damage is a short, specific sequence of actions, and knowing who to call next, a plumber, a restoration company, or both, matters just as much as what you do in the first 60 seconds.

## What Do You Do in the First 60 Seconds?

Shut off the water at your main valve, then cut power to any outlets or fixtures standing in water. Those two steps stop the leak from spreading and remove the biggest immediate safety hazard while you call for help.

The main shutoff is usually near the street-facing wall, the water meter box, or inside the garage. In older Ventura craftsman homes built before the 1960s, it's sometimes behind the water heater or down in a crawl space, so it's worth locating it today, before an emergency, not during one. Once the water is off, move anything valuable out of the water's path and use towels to soak up what's pooling on hard flooring. Don't step into standing water near outlets or appliances.

## Who Do You Call First, a Plumber or a Restoration Company?

Call a plumber to physically repair or replace the broken section of pipe, and call a water damage restoration company to start extracting water and drying the structure. These are two separate jobs, and waiting for one to finish before starting the other costs you time you don't have.

The plumber's job is narrow: find the failure point, cut out and replace the damaged section, and confirm there's no further leak behind the wall. The restoration side is everything else: pulling standing water, removing flooring or baseboards that won't dry in place, and setting commercial air movers and dehumidifiers. Water from a burst supply line typically starts as Category 1 (clean) water under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), but it doesn't stay that way. The longer it sits against drywall, subfloor, and insulation, the more likely it is to need treatment as a more contaminated category, which is part of why speed matters more than it might seem.

## What Happens in the First 24 Hours After the Water Is Shut Off?

The first 24 hours are about getting the structure dry, not about repairs. A restoration crew extracts standing water, removes saturated materials that won't dry in place, and runs industrial dehumidifiers and air movers continuously.

Mold can start growing on wet building materials within [24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), which is the real clock everyone is racing. Technicians use moisture meters behind drywall and under flooring to track progress daily, and full structural drying usually takes three to five days depending on the materials affected and how long water sat before anyone arrived. Ventura's coastal marine layer adds humidity that slows natural air-drying compared to inland spots like Thousand Oaks or Simi Valley, which is one reason mechanical dehumidification matters more here than it might elsewhere.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover a Burst Pipe in Ventura?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe, but they exclude damage from a gradual leak or long-term wear and tear. Insurers want to see the pipe failed suddenly, not that it had been dripping for months before anyone noticed.

Document everything before cleanup starts: photos of the standing water, the damaged materials, and the failed pipe section if the plumber removes it. Ask your restoration crew for a written moisture-mapping report, since that documentation is what adjusters use to support a claim. Homeowners also have a duty to mitigate further damage, so starting drying promptly isn't just good practice, it protects the claim itself. For a fuller breakdown of what's typically covered, see the [Insurance Information Institute's guide on homeowners coverage](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) and this site's own [plain-English guide to water damage coverage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).
