## What should you ask before hiring a crawl space encapsulation company in Ventura?

Ask for a CSLB license number, a written moisture reading from the day of the quote, and a plain answer about what happens if they find mold once the old insulation comes down. Those three answers tell you more than any sales pitch.

You can [verify any California contractor's license status directly with the CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx) before you sign anything. Also ask what vapor barrier mil thickness is included, whether the quote covers sealing vents and crawl space doors, and whether a dehumidifier is part of the scope or a separate add-on. A company that hedges on the moisture question, or quotes a price before setting foot under the house, is skipping the step that actually determines whether the fix holds.

## Should you hire a restoration company or a crawl space specialist?

A company that holds IICRC water-damage and mold certifications is usually the safer pick over a single-trade vapor-barrier installer, because crawl space moisture problems are rarely just a vapor barrier problem.

Raised-foundation homes across Ventura, Oxnard, and Camarillo often develop crawl space moisture from a combination of grading, irrigation runoff, and a vented foundation that was never built for California's current energy codes. A specialist who only installs liner can seal in a moisture or mold problem that was already there. A firm trained to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) and mold protocols can assess the moisture source, document conditions, and remediate mold under the same contract instead of handing you off to a second company mid-project. ## How do you know the moisture problem was actually fixed?

You know it's fixed when the contractor hands you matched moisture meter readings from before the job and again after the liner and any drying equipment go in, not just a verbal "it's dry now."

A pin-type or pinless moisture meter reading on exposed floor joists and subfloor should come down into a stable, low range after encapsulation, and a reputable crew records that number both times rather than estimating. If standing water or active leaks caused the problem, the crew should also confirm the water source is a clean, gray, or black water category under IICRC guidelines, since that changes what has to be removed versus dried in place. Ask to see the readings in writing, not just hear them. If the home already had a flooded crawl space event, our post on [who to call for a flooded crawl space in Ventura, CA](/blog/who-to-call-flooded-crawl-space-ventura-ca/) covers what documentation that situation specifically requires.

## What vapor barrier thickness and warranty should you expect?

Most crawl space encapsulation jobs use a vapor barrier between 10 and 20 mil, with 12 mil being a common mid-grade standard for residential crawl spaces that aren't used for storage or foot traffic.

Thinner 6 mil liner is a budget option that tears easily and usually isn't worth the lower price. Heavier 20 mil liner costs more but holds up better in crawl spaces with debris, rodents, or frequent access for plumbing work. Ask what the warranty actually covers: material defects only, or labor to repair a tear from a future plumber or electrician crawling through. A warranty that only covers the material and not a return visit isn't much of a warranty in practice.

## What are the red flags in a crawl space encapsulation quote?

The biggest red flag is a flat price quoted sight unseen, before anyone has actually been under the house to measure square footage, vent count, or existing moisture damage.

Other warning signs: no CSLB license number on the proposal, pressure to sign same-day for a "today only" discount, a scope that doesn't mention sealing vents or crawl space access doors, and vague language about mold ("we'll handle it if we see it") instead of a defined remediation process tied to the [ANSI/IICRC S520 mold remediation standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/). If standing moisture has been a long-term issue, also ask whether the quote includes a dehumidifier, since a sealed crawl space without humidity control can still grow mold even with a new liner.

Once you've compared a few quotes against these criteria, request a written scope that lists moisture readings, vapor barrier mil thickness, vent sealing, and warranty terms side by side.
