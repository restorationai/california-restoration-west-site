**TL;DR:** When rain leaks into a Ventura home, call a water damage restoration company first, not a roofer. The roofer eventually fixes the hole that let the rain in, but by the time a roof repair gets scheduled, wet drywall, insulation and framing are already on a 24 to 48 hour clock toward mold. Document the damage with photos, then call your insurance company after mitigation is already underway.

If you're standing in your living room right now watching a brown ring spread across the ceiling, or water is running down the inside of a window frame during one of Ventura's winter atmospheric river storms, you don't need a lecture on roof maintenance. You need to know who picks up the phone first. The answer depends on what's actually happening: active water intrusion needs extraction and drying immediately, while the hole in the roof itself is a separate, slower-moving problem.

## Who should I call first when rain leaks into my house in Ventura?

Call a water damage restoration company first, because stopping and drying the water is the time-sensitive part. A roofer fixes the entry point, but that repair can usually wait a day or two once the leak is contained; the water already inside your walls and insulation cannot wait.

For storm intrusion anywhere from midtown Ventura to the Ventura Avenue corridor, the first call should go to whoever can get moisture meters and air movers on-site before the drywall has absorbed water for another full day.

## Should I call a roofer or a water damage company first?

Call the water damage company first for extraction and drying, then line up a roofer separately for the permanent fix. These are two different trades solving two different problems on two different timelines.

A roofer's job is to find the failure point, a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing seal, a clogged valley, and seal it so it doesn't leak again. That's not urgent in the next two hours; it's urgent in the next day or two, especially if more rain is forecast. What *is* urgent is the water already sitting in your ceiling cavity, soaked into blown-in insulation, or wicking up the base of a drywall panel. A restoration crew can place a temporary tarp or containment while the roof repair gets scheduled, but their real job is pulling moisture out of the structure before it does secondary damage. To be direct about scope: the roof patch itself isn't a service a water damage company performs, and you should expect them to say so plainly and hand you to a roofer for that piece, while they handle everything on the water side.

## What happens in the first 24 to 48 hours after a rain leak?

The first 24 to 48 hours determine whether this stays a drying job or turns into a mold remediation job. What happens in that window is moisture mapping, extraction, and setting up controlled drying, not demolition.

A technician uses a moisture meter to find exactly how far the water traveled behind walls and under flooring, since rain intrusion often spreads sideways along a top plate or down inside a stud bay far from where you see the stain. Standing water gets extracted first. Then come air movers positioned to create directional airflow across wet surfaces, paired with a dehumidifier sized to the affected area, following the same drying principles laid out in the IICRC S500 standard. Drywall that stayed wet less than 48 hours can often be dried in place; drywall and insulation saturated longer usually needs to come out. For the fuller walkthrough of that sequence, see [what to do in the first 24 hours after water damage](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/).

## When do I call my insurance company?

Call your insurer after you've documented the damage and mitigation is already starting, not before. Most homeowners policies cover sudden water intrusion from a storm, but they expect you to act promptly to limit the damage once it starts.

Take photos and a short video of the ceiling stain, the window sill, and any standing water before anything gets moved or dried. Note the date and approximate start time of the leak. California's Department of Insurance publishes [residential insurance guides](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/03-res/) that explain how claims documentation and the adjuster process generally work, which is worth a skim before that first call. A restoration company that's already on-site can usually provide a moisture reading log and scope of work that becomes part of your claim file. For the fuller list of what to gather, see the [storm damage insurance claim checklist](/blog/storm-damage-insurance-claim-checklist/).

## How fast does mold grow after a rain leak?

Mold can begin colonizing wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours under the right temperature and humidity conditions, which is why speed matters more than perfection in the first response. The EPA's guidance on [mold cleanup in the home](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) treats that window as the practical line between a drying job and a remediation job.

A ceiling that stayed damp over a weekend because the leak went unnoticed, or because the first available roofer couldn't come out for three days, is a very different scope than a leak caught and dried same-day. If you're past that window already, or you're noticing a musty smell a few days after a storm, that's a different conversation. See [how soon mold grows after water damage](/blog/how-soon-does-mold-grow-after-water-damage/) for the signs to check before you decide whether you need a mold inspection in addition to drying.

If the leak just started, the priority is simple: get water extraction and drying moving today, then schedule the roof repair once the forecast clears. Ventura's rainy season runs roughly November through March, and homes with original 1960s and '70s roofing in older midtown neighborhoods tend to show these leaks first once the atmospheric rivers start coming through.
