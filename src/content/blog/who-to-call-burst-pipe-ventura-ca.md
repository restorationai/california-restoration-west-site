## Who do I call first when a pipe bursts in my house?

Shut off the water at the main valve before you call anyone. Most Ventura homes have the shutoff near the street-side hose bib or in the garage; turn it clockwise until it stops.

Once the water is off, you're making two separate calls, not one. A restoration company handles the standing water, the wet drywall, and the drying equipment, because every hour that water sits, it's soaking further into framing and baseboards. A plumber handles the actual pipe, locating the break and cutting it out or re-piping the section. Calling only a plumber and skipping extraction is how a one-day repair turns into a mold problem three weeks later.

## Do I call a plumber or a restoration company first?

Call the restoration company as soon as the water is shut off and the leak has clearly stopped; call a plumber immediately if water is still actively flowing and you can't find or reach the shutoff. If the break is spraying from inside a wall and the main valve doesn't stop it (a rare but real scenario with old galvanized supply lines common in Ventura's pre-1960s housing stock), that's an emergency plumbing call before anything else.

If you smell gas, see water near an electrical panel, or the water is coming from a sewer line rather than a supply line, stop and call 911 or your utility company first. Those are hazard calls, not restoration calls. For straightforward burst-pipe water damage, extraction comes before repair, not after, because standing water doesn't wait for a plumber's schedule.

## What are the common burst-pipe spots in Ventura homes?

Three spots account for most of the calls we see: inside an exterior or shared wall, under a raised-foundation crawl space, and within a slab foundation. Each one changes how the water spreads and how fast you need equipment on site.

A break inside a wall, often where a supply line runs behind a kitchen or bathroom, soaks the wall cavity and the flooring at the base before it's visible. Homes on raised foundations along the Ventura Avenue corridor and in the hillside neighborhoods are common for this because older copper and galvanized lines run through tighter, less-insulated wall cavities.

A break under the house in a crawl space is easy to miss for days, since the water pools under the subfloor instead of showing up on a visible floor. Musty smell, a soft or spongy spot in the flooring, or a spike in your water bill are usually the first signs. Crawl space moisture that sits untreated is exactly the setup that leads to subfloor rot and mold, which is why [crawl space encapsulation](/services/crawl-space-encapsulation/) often follows a crawl space pipe repair in older Ventura homes.

A slab leak, common in Pierpont and other beach-adjacent neighborhoods built on slab-on-grade foundations, pushes water up through the concrete and into flooring from below. These are harder to diagnose without a plumber's leak-detection equipment, and the restoration side focuses on drying the slab and any flooring that's already delaminated.

## What happens when a restoration crew answers the call?

The first visit is extraction, moisture mapping, and setting equipment, not demolition. A technician uses a moisture meter to map how far the water traveled behind walls and under flooring, pulls standing water with a truck-mounted or portable extractor, and sets air movers and a dehumidifier to begin structural drying under the [ANSI/IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) water damage standard.

Materials that can be dried in place usually are; wet drywall below the waterline, soaked baseboard, and saturated carpet pad are the materials most often removed, because drying them in place risks trapped moisture and mold growth. If your situation calls for broader water removal and drying across the whole affected area, that's the scope covered under [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) and [emergency water removal](/services/emergency-water-removal/) rather than a single-room extraction.

## Will homeowners insurance pay for this?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water discharge, which is exactly what a burst pipe is. The extraction, drying, and any resulting drywall or flooring replacement from that sudden event are typically covered, subject to your deductible.

What's usually not covered is the plumbing repair itself and any damage tied to a pipe that was already leaking slowly before it failed. Insurers look for signs of neglect or a long-term, undetected leak, so document the damage with photos before cleanup starts and keep your restoration invoice for the claim. Coverage details vary by policy, so this isn't a guarantee; the [California Department of Insurance's residential claims guides](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/03-res/) are a solid plain-language reference for what a standard policy typically includes. Our [guide on burst pipe emergency steps](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) walks through the full documentation checklist if you want it before you make the first call.
