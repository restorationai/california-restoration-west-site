## Is This a Restoration Call or a Plumber Call First?

Call a plumber first only if water is actively flowing from a pipe, fixture, or water heater that hasn't been shut off yet. Stopping the source always comes before drying anything out.

Once the water is off, or if the water came from outside (storm runoff, a yard that drains toward the foundation, a cracked vent well), the next call is a restoration company, not a plumber. Plumbers fix the pipe; they generally don't own extraction pumps, air movers, or commercial dehumidifiers sized for a crawl space, and they're not set up to document moisture readings for an insurance claim.

If you smell gas, see exposed wiring sitting in water, or the panel box is anywhere near the wet area, that's a utility or electrician call before anyone goes under the house. Standing water and electrical components are a genuine shock and fire hazard, and no restoration crew should enter until power to that area is confirmed off.

## What Happens When You Call California Restoration West

The first call is a short conversation about what's wet, how it started, and whether the water source is stopped. Have the approximate size of the crawl space, how long water has been standing, and your insurance company's name ready if you have it.

A technician then inspects the crawl space with a moisture meter and thermal imaging where needed, checking how far water traveled under the subfloor and whether it reached insulation, ductwork, or stored items. Extraction comes next: submersible pumps and truck-mounted extractors pull standing water out, then commercial air movers and dehumidifiers are set to bring the crawl space back to a normal moisture level. ## Why a Flooded Ventura Crawl Space Needs Fast Extraction and Drying

A flooded crawl space dries slowly because it's enclosed, poorly ventilated, and usually has dirt or gravel flooring that holds moisture long after the surface looks dry. Left alone, that trapped moisture keeps wicking up into subfloor framing for weeks.

Mold can begin growing on wet wood and insulation within [24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold), so the clock matters more than it looks like it should. Wet fiberglass insulation generally needs to come out rather than be dried in place; it holds water and loses its R-value once saturated. Soaked cardboard, stored boxes, or old carpet remnants under the house usually get discarded for the same reason.

Once the space is fully dry and any mold growth has been addressed through [mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/), many Ventura homeowners choose [crawl space encapsulation](/services/crawl-space-encapsulation/): sealing the vents, lining the walls and floor with a vapor barrier, and sometimes adding a dehumidifier. For a property near the river bottom or with a history of repeat flooding, encapsulation is the step that keeps the next storm from putting you back in this exact spot.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover a Flooded Crawl Space in Ventura?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, such as a burst supply line or a failed water heater, when it floods a crawl space. They generally exclude gradual seepage and long-term moisture under wear-and-tear and maintenance clauses, since insurers expect homeowners to catch and fix slow leaks.

Groundwater intrusion and flooding from heavy rain or runoff are different: those are flood losses, and a standard homeowners policy doesn't cover them. That requires a separate flood insurance policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer](https://www.floodsmart.gov/). Sewer or drain backups and sump pump failures are different again: those typically need a water backup (sewer/sump) endorsement added to the policy, and without it, that kind of loss is usually denied. Check your [policy's declarations page](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance) for both items before assuming either one is covered.
